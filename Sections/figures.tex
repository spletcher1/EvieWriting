\begin{figure}[p]

\caption{\textbf{EB manipulation does not impair climbing ability.} Height reached by 9 sec after drop (top plots of each panel) and total up distance climbed in 30 sec after drop (bottom plots). 
Interaction (Light $\times$ Genotype) p-values were derived from 2-way ANOVAs. 
Benjamini-Hochberg correction was applied within each sex and driver and across time points and climbing metrics. 
Points represent average of 3 trials per individual. 
n = 23-28.
\textbf{(A-B)} One week of activation has no effect on ER2 and ER2/ER4 males and females. 
Female ER2/ER4 flies exhibit improved climbing by both metrics after one month of activation; ER2 females, ER2 males, and ER2/ER4 males exhibit no change after one month. 
\textbf{(C)} ER4d>GtACR1 males, but not females, show improved climbing ability after both one week and five weeks of inhibition. 
}
\label{fig:ddropfig}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[p]
\centering
\includegraphics[scale=1]{Figs/FLIC_Fig.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{FLIC captures distinct valence states induced by EB manipulation.} 
Sucrose interaction p-values were derived from the Genotype $\times$ Linkage interaction term of a linear mixed model (Interactions ~ Genotype * Linkage + (1|Group), lmer) with yoked pairs modeled as a random intercept.
Benjamini-Hochberg correction was applied within each sex and driver and across time points. 
\textbf{(A)} A "trigger" fly (top chamber) controlled optogenetic light for itself and a yoked control (bottom) by touching a 10\% sucrose (S10) well. 
\textbf{(B)} ER2 activation was aversive in light-naïve flies and in flies pre-treated with light for 1 month. n = 9-18. 
\textbf{(C)} ER4d activation was transiently aversive in flies pre-treated with light for 1 week. n = 13-18. 
\textbf{(D)} After correction, ER2/ER4 flies were indistinguishable from yoked controls. Before correction, light-naïve flies found the well rewarding (p = 0.020). n = 10-18.
}
\label{fig:flicfig}
\end{figure}

\caption{\textbf{Valence of EB manipulation is sex-, circuit-, and context-dependent.} 
\textbf{(A)} Light-naïve flies (n = 34-36) were placed in lanes with a dark side and an illuminated side (red light, 48 Hz) and subjected to a two-phase protocol: 1) a 10-min dark acclimation period; 2) a 60-min treatment of 5 sec on/5 sec off. Reported p-values for Preference Index correspond to Genotype $\times$ Phase interaction term. 
\textbf{(B)} ER2/ER4>Chr females and males strongly preferred lit side of arena. 
\textbf{(C)} ER2>Chr females, but not males, preferred dark side. 
\textbf{(D)} ER4d>Chr females preferred lit side, but showed decreased light preference relative to controls. Males preferred dark side.
}
\label{fig:arenafig}
\end{figure}
